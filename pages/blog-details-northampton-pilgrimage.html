The Northampton Pilgrimage:
A Cobbler's Mecca Revisited

Tariq Hussain
· 20 August 2024 · 9 min read · Heritage
Northampton Pilgrimage Bench Made Heritage

Why We Still Go

Every August, IronStitch closes for three days and drives north to Northampton. Not for nostalgia — for calibration. In London we repair what Northampton made; to repair it rightly, we must watch it being made — the clicker's knife angle, the inseamer's stitch twist, the finisher's iron temperature.

This year we apprenticed at a one-man workshop behind the old Drill Hall — a bench maker who produces 40 pairs annually, entirely by hand, no Goodyear machine. His waiting list is 4 years.

"You think London is fast-paced until you see a closer skive six quarters in eight minutes without a nick. Speed is not haste when it is learned." — Tariq Hussain, Pilgrimage Notes 2024

Beyond the Factories

Tourists visit Crockett and Church's. Pilgrims visit the ancillary trades that keep them alive.

  • ①Soles Direct: Where oak bark tanned bends from Devon are sorted into sole leather grades — A for sole, B for heel.
  • ②John Anderson: Last makers carving the final wooden last house in the UK — maple and beech, guarded like heirlooms.
  • ③B. Green: Eyelet and hook makers stamping brass since 1898; their antique press still runs.

What We Bring Home

We return with hides selected by eye, not catalogue, and with techniques: the bench maker's method of wetting and hammering toe puffs for sharper shape, and his trick of warming beeswax edge dressing to 40°C before applying — it penetrates deeper.

We also return with humility. In London we are masters; in Northampton we are students. That ratio is healthy.

How to Make Your Own Pilgrimage

Start at the Shoe Museum (free, 2 hours), then book the Edward Green factory tour (£35, Fridays). Finish at The Hide pub on Wellingborough Road — cobblers have drunk there since 1912. Ask for the corner table.

Wear comfortable shoes; you will be invited to try lasting — and you will fail charmingly.

The Exchange Programme

IronStitch hosts a Northampton apprentice in London each winter and sends a London apprentice north each summer — keeping the craft circular.

  1. Summer pilgrimage and technique exchange
  2. Material sourcing from tanneries and last makers
  3. Winter hosting of Northampton apprentice
  4. Public talk at IronStitch on return — next is September
