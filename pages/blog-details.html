The Goodyear Welt: Why the World's Finest Shoes
Still Use a 140-Year-Old Method

Clara Ironsmith
· 15 November 2024 · 8 min read · Craft & Technique
Goodyear Welt Shoe Construction Heritage Repair & Restoration

What is a Goodyear Welt?

In 1869, Charles Goodyear Jr. (son of the vulcanised rubber pioneer) invented a machine that could replicate, at scale, the laborious hand-sewn welt construction that cobblers had practiced for centuries. The principle was elegant: rather than attaching the sole directly to the upper, a thin strip of leather or rubber — the "welt" — is first sewn around the perimeter of the upper, and the sole is then sewn or cemented to the welt separately.

This seemingly minor engineering decision has profound consequences. Because the welt acts as an intermediary, the sole can be entirely removed and replaced without disturbing the upper at all. A Goodyear-welted shoe, properly maintained, can theoretically be resoled a dozen times — making it not just a shoe, but an heirloom.

"A well-made Goodyear-welted shoe is not a purchase. It's a forty-year relationship between the wearer and a craftsman." — Edmund Ironsmith, founder, 1968

The Three Layers of a Welted Shoe

A true Goodyear-welted shoe is built in three distinct layers, each sewn to the next in sequence:

  • ①The Upper: The leather body of the shoe, lasting tape stitched around the insole board to create the internal upper structure.
  • ②The Welt: A 10–12mm strip of leather (or rubber in military/outdoor constructions) sewn around the perimeter in a single, continuous seam — the inseam.
  • ③The Sole: The outsole, stitched to the welt via the outseam, then finished, burnished and edge-dressed.

Why Does It Enable Superior Repair?

The genius of the construction, from a cobbler's perspective, is the structural independence of each layer. When the sole wears through — as all soles eventually do — it can be removed cleanly at the outseam without touching the upper. A new sole is shaped, stitched and finished, and the shoe is functionally new again.

By contrast, a Blake-stitched or cemented shoe is attached directly: upper to sole. To remove the sole, the cobbler must cut through stitching that passes through the insole and risks damaging the upper. It can be done — and we do it here at IronStitch — but it's a more invasive process.

The Cork Filling

One detail that shoe lovers never see, but always feel, is the cork filling packed between the insole and outsole of a Goodyear-welted shoe. Over time, this cork compresses and moulds to the exact contour of the wearer's foot — a completely bespoke fit, grown over years of wear.

When we resole a welted shoe at IronStitch, we always replace the cork filling with fresh material and pack it carefully before stitching the new sole. This restores the cushioning and ensures the shoe will continue to mould correctly over its next lifetime.

Recognising a Goodyear-Welted Shoe

You can identify a welted shoe by looking at the side profile between the upper and the sole. If you see a visible ridge of material — the welt — running around the perimeter of the shoe, it's welted. You may also see two rows of stitching: the inseam (holding upper to welt) and the outseam (holding welt to sole).

The most revered Goodyear-welted shoemakers include Edward Green, John Lobb, Crockett & Jones, Tricker's and G.H. Bass — all British or deeply rooted in the British tradition. Italian makers like Berluti and Santoni also use the construction for their top-tier models.

The IronStitch Approach to Goodyear Resoling

At IronStitch, we resole approximately 60 Goodyear-welted shoes per week. Our process is as follows:

  1. Remove the old outsole at the outseam with a lasting pincers and skiver
  2. Inspect the welt and midsole for damage; replace if necessary
  3. Remove old cork filling and clean the insole cavity
  4. Pack fresh cork filling to original depth
  5. Cut and skive the new sole to correct thickness and shape
  6. Stitch the outseam with waxed thread at the original stitch count
  7. Trim, burnish and edge-dress the sole to match original colour
  8. Condition and polish the upper
